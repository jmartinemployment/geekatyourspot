The Cost of Manual Automated Payment Execution

For small businesses, managing Automated Payment Execution can quickly become a tangled web of inefficiencies. As a business grows, the complexity of handling payments increases. Invoices often sit in inboxes, waiting for someone to notice them. This delay can stretch payment cycles unnecessarily, leading to missed discounts and strained cash flow. The process is reactive, not proactive, which means payments are often executed ad hoc, with no strategic oversight.

Approval processes are another major pain point. In many small businesses, approval is informal and often reliant on the owner, who becomes a bottleneck. This not only slows down the process but also creates a lack of accountability, as there's no formal record of who approved what. This informal system can lead to errors, such as duplicate payments, because there's no control mechanism in place to catch these mistakes before they happen.

The separation of duties is often weak, with the same employee entering bills and releasing payments. This lack of separation increases the risk of fraud and errors. Payment timing becomes a guessing game, leading to payments being made too early or too late. This not only affects cash flow but also the business's ability to take advantage of early payment discounts.

Moreover, the status of vendor payments is often disconnected from the accounting system. This disconnection means that leadership lacks a reliable snapshot of approved-but-unpaid liabilities, making financial planning difficult. Multiple people using the same bank login or sharing approval authority through email further complicates the issue, as it undermines security and accountability.
