Manual tracking is where the process breaks down first. Sales flow through Shopify, Amazon, or a direct website. Product classification is another trap. States apply different rules to each type of product or service. A SaaS subscription might be exempt in one state and fully taxable in another. Apparel, supplements, and digital goods all have their own quirks. Most small teams assign generic product codes and hope for the best. But when a state audits, they look for precise classification. Misclassifying a product means under- or over-collecting tax, either of which can trigger an audit or a demand for back payments. Regulatory notices are the next hurdle. States send compliance letters by mail or email, each with a deadline and a required response. For a business without a dedicated compliance officer, these notices pile up. Some are routine, but others are warnings of missed filings, pending audits, or changes in the law. Missing or misreading a notice can mean a penalty or a forced registration in a state the business was not prepared for. The owner spends hours deciphering the language, searching online for answers, or calling state agencies that may not provide clear guidance. If the notice is not resolved, the business risks escalating penalties or even a sales tax audit.

Timing is unforgiving. Once a business crosses a nexus threshold, the clock starts ticking. States expect registration and collection to begin immediately. Starting late, even by a few weeks, creates a gap where sales tax should have been collected but was not. The business is then liable for the tax, even if it was never collected from customers. This can quickly add up to thousands of dollars in unplanned costs, especially for fast-growing companies that cross multiple thresholds in a short period.

Reconciling sales data is the final headache. At year-end, the business must merge sales from multiple channels, each with its own format and level of detail. Manual reconciliation is slow and prone to error. A missing transaction or a duplicated entry can throw off filings and open the door to audit risk. The business spends valuable time cleaning up data instead of focusing on growth. Even with tax software, most tools require the business to configure integrations, map products, and monitor for errors. When something breaks, a plugin fails, a rule changes, or a notice is missed, the business is on its own to fix it.
