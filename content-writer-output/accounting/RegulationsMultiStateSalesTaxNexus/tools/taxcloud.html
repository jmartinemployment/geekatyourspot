What It Costs to Keep Doing Multi-State Sales Tax by Hand

A South Florida business owner lands a new account in New York, and suddenly the workload triples. Crossing a state line is not just a sales win, it is the start of a new compliance burden. Each state sets its own economic nexus rules: some trigger at $100,000 in sales, others at 200 transactions, and a few at higher thresholds. The problem is, these thresholds are easy to cross and hard to track. The moment a business sells enough in a new state, it is legally required to register, collect, and file sales tax there. Miss the trigger and penalties follow, even if the mistake was just a missed transaction count.

Manual tracking fails because the rules do not stand still. States update their thresholds, change how they count transactions, and add new requirements every year. A business that sells through both Shopify and Amazon faces an even harder task: each platform reports only its own sales. That means the owner spends hours every month trying to reconcile two sets of numbers, hoping nothing falls through the cracks. The risk is not just a late return, it is the chance of missing a state entirely, and owing back taxes on sales that were never reported. That is not a theoretical problem; it is the reality for small teams who sell across state lines.

Even when the team manages to track the right states, the next trap is rate calculation. Many try to use basic zip code tables built into their ecommerce platform. But local sales tax rates change at the city, county, and even district level. Home-rule states like Colorado and Louisiana set their own rules, and rates can vary from one block to the next. Using a flat rate or an out-of-date table means collecting too little tax, which creates liability at audit time, or overcharging customers and risking lost sales. A business that thinks it is covered because Shopify or WooCommerce collects tax at checkout is often wrong: those platforms handle only their own transactions, and do not keep up with every local change.

Registration is another point of failure. It is not enough to collect tax; a business must be registered with the state before it can legally do so. Failing to register before collecting is tax fraud, even if the error was unintentional. The registration process itself is manual, varies by state, and is easy to miss when the team is already stretched thin. Once registered, the obligation does not go away in slow months. States expect returns to be filed on schedule, even if sales drop to zero. Missing a filing, especially a zero return, triggers automatic penalties and interest. A team that can handle two states with spreadsheets quickly finds itself overwhelmed at five or more, where the calendar of due dates becomes a risk in itself.

Exemption certificates add yet another layer. Selling B2B means managing exemption certificates for out-of-state buyers. Accepting an invalid or expired certificate can leave the business on the hook for uncollected tax during an audit. Tracking, validating, and storing these certificates is a manual process for most small teams, and one mistake can mean an audit bill that wipes out months of profit.

The owner who started the week celebrating a big order ends it buried in spreadsheets, chasing down missing data, and worrying about which state might come calling next. The cost is not just the hours lost to compliance, but the opportunity cost of not growing the business. Every hour spent reconciling sales reports or updating tax tables is an hour not spent on sales, customer service, or product development. The risk is not just a fine, but stalled growth and missed opportunities. This is the reality of manual multi-state sales tax compliance for small teams in South Florida.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I have nexus in Florida?

You have nexus in Florida if your business has a physical presence in the state, such as an office, warehouse, employees, or inventory stored in an Amazon FBA center. You also have nexus if your taxable remote sales into Florida went over $100,000 in the previous calendar year. Florida’s threshold only counts taxable sales, so exempt items like SaaS do not apply. Marketplace sales where the facilitator collects tax are also excluded. TaxCloud’s nexus tracking helps you monitor your exposure across all states in real time.

Does Florida charge sales tax on shipping?

Florida does not charge sales tax on separately stated shipping charges for delivering tangible personal property. If shipping is bundled into the product price or combined with handling charges that include taxable items, the entire charge is taxable.

What happens if I miss a Florida sales tax filing deadline?

If you miss a Florida sales tax filing deadline, the state charges a late penalty of 10% of the tax due (with a minimum of $50), plus interest. This penalty applies even if you had zero sales for that period, you must still file a zero-return. Electronic filers have until the 20th of the month, while paper filers must file by the 1st.

Does Florida tax SaaS and digital products?

Florida does not tax SaaS or digital products like eBooks, music downloads, or streaming services. SaaS delivered electronically is treated as an intangible service and is not taxable. However, prewritten software delivered on physical media (like a CD or USB drive) is taxable at the standard 6% state rate plus any county surtax.

Do I need to collect Florida sales tax on Amazon FBA sales?

If you store inventory in a Florida FBA warehouse, you have physical nexus and must register with the Department of Revenue. Amazon collects and remits tax for your marketplace sales, but you are still responsible for collecting tax on sales made through your own website (like Shopify or WooCommerce) to Florida customers. Your Amazon marketplace sales do not count toward your $100,000 economic nexus threshold.
