The Challenges of Manual AP Workflows

Small and medium-sized businesses often grapple with managing accounts payable (AP) processes that are scattered across various platforms like email, paper, spreadsheets, and accounting systems. This lack of a unified system leads to inefficiencies and errors. The workflow lacks a clear path from invoice receipt to payment authorization, causing delays and poor cash visibility. Business owners find themselves constantly chasing approvals, which not only increases administrative workload but also reduces the time available for more strategic tasks like reconciliation and cash planning.

One of the major issues is that approvals often get stuck with a single person. This could be the business owner or a department head who is frequently unavailable due to travel or other commitments. The result is late payments, late fees, and strained supplier relationships. Additionally, without a single view of invoice status, staff members face confusion over responsibility, leading to duplicate follow-ups and payment delays.

Invoices often arrive through various channels, including email, paper mail, and vendor portals, which can result in lost or misrouted bills. Unstructured email-based approvals further complicate the process, as approvals can be implied in a thread or given without the necessary documentation attached. This lack of accountability can lead to rework and difficulty in proving who approved what and when. Furthermore, unclear approval rules mean employees are unsure about which invoices require approval, leading to inconsistent control over financial processes.

The workflow breaks down during exceptions, such as when an invoice has a wrong amount or is missing a receipt. Without a defined escalation route, payments sit unresolved, and staff make ad hoc decisions. Limited mobile and out-of-office approval capabilities further stall the process, especially when key people are unavailable. As businesses grow, the need for multiple approvals creates bottlenecks, extending cycle times from days to weeks, particularly near month-end. Companies often resort to giving broad access to pay bills to too many people, which compromises the separation of duties and increases the time spent chasing approvers.

To address these challenges, a structured AP workflow is essential. Ramp offers a solution with its Bill Pay feature, positioning it as a standalone AP solution that includes invoice capture, purchase order (PO) matching, approval workflows, vendor management, and payment capabilities. By routing bills automatically to the right approver based on business rules, Ramp supports separation of duties through defined roles and permissions.
