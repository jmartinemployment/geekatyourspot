The Challenges of Manual Accounts Payable Processes

Many small and medium-sized businesses struggle with managing their accounts payable (AP) processes manually. The traditional approach involves invoices arriving through multiple channels like email, paper, and vendor portals. These invoices then sit in an owner's or manager's inbox waiting for informal approval. This lack of a centralized system leads to a host of issues that can hinder business operations.

Without a structured approval workflow, businesses face delayed approvals and poor cash visibility. Invoices can easily be overlooked or lost, resulting in late payments and potential late fees. When approvals are stuck with one individual, such as an owner or department lead, the process can become bottlenecked if that person is unavailable. This results in rushed decisions and strained supplier relationships.

The administrative burden on bookkeepers is another significant issue. They often find themselves repeatedly chasing approvers through emails or calls, which increases their workload and leaves less time for essential tasks like reconciliation and cash planning. Moreover, the lack of a single view of invoice status can lead to confusion and duplicate follow-ups, further complicating the AP process.

Invoices arriving through various channels can be misplaced or misdirected, leading to delays in processing. Email-based approvals are often unstructured, with approvals sometimes given verbally or without the necessary documentation. This lack of accountability can result in rework and difficulty in proving who approved what and when.

Additionally, unclear approval rules and the absence of a defined escalation route for exceptions can cause payments to sit unresolved. This inconsistency in handling invoices can lead to some bills being over-reviewed while others are paid without sufficient oversight. All these challenges highlight the need for a centralized, automated system to manage AP processes more efficiently.
