The Challenges of Manual Accounts Payable Processes

For small and medium-sized businesses, managing accounts payable (AP) can be a complex and time-consuming process, especially when it involves multiple entities, international suppliers, and varied compliance requirements. Typically, these businesses rely on a mix of email, spreadsheets, and manual input to track and approve invoices. This fragmented approach often leads to delays in approval, lack of visibility into cash flow, and increased risk of errors.

One common issue is the dependency on a single individual for approvals. When the owner or a key manager is unavailable, invoices can pile up, leading to late payments and potential supplier friction. Meanwhile, bookkeepers spend excessive time following up on approvals, reducing their capacity to focus on more strategic tasks like reconciliation and cash planning.

Additionally, without a centralized view of invoice status, businesses struggle to track where each invoice stands in the approval process. This lack of transparency can result in duplicate follow-ups, confusion, and ultimately, payment delays. Invoices arriving through various channels such as email, paper mail, and vendor portals further complicate the process, increasing the likelihood of invoices being lost or misdirected.

The absence of structured approval rules adds another layer of complexity. Employees may be unclear about which invoices require approval, who is responsible for budget oversight, and what approval thresholds are in place. This inconsistency can lead to some invoices being over-scrutinized while others slip through without adequate oversight.

Moreover, when exceptions arise—such as discrepancies in invoice amounts or missing documentation—there is often no defined process for resolution. This can cause payments to stall, as staff make ad hoc decisions without clear guidance. The need for approvers to be at their desks to access shared drives or systems further delays approvals, especially when key personnel are unavailable.

For businesses trying to manage global or multi-entity AP within a domestic-focused system, the challenges are even greater. Supplier onboarding can be inconsistent, with incomplete tax and banking data, unclear approvals, and manual payment checks. These issues can delay financial close processes due to the need for extensive reconciliation and exception handling.

The Mechanics of Tipalti's Automation

Tipalti stands out as a comprehensive solution for automating the accounts payable process, particularly for small to medium-sized businesses looking to enhance efficiency and accuracy. The platform's architecture is designed to streamline the entire procure-to-pay cycle, reducing the reliance on manual data entry and minimizing errors. This is achieved through several key features that integrate seamlessly into existing workflows.

One of the core strengths of Tipalti is its automated purchase order (PO) matching capability. This feature includes two-way and three-way matching against purchase orders and goods receipts, using pre-set rules and algorithms. This automation not only reduces the time spent on manual verification but also ensures that payments are made only for goods or services that have been accurately billed and received. By automating the matching process, businesses can prevent overspending and maintain tighter control over their budgets.

Tipalti also excels in exception handling, leveraging artificial intelligence to identify discrepancies between invoices, POs, and receipts. When such discrepancies are detected, they are flagged for additional review, ensuring that only compliant and accurate transactions proceed to payment. This intelligent handling of exceptions significantly reduces the risk of errors and ensures compliance with internal policies.

Data extraction is another critical component of Tipalti's system. Utilizing optical character recognition (OCR) technology, the platform extracts relevant invoice data by line item, which is then used to populate electronic invoices. This automated data capture reduces manual entry errors and speeds up the invoice processing time, freeing up the accounts payable team to focus on more strategic tasks.

The platform also offers sophisticated reporting and analytics tools. These provide insights into exception rates, validations, and the performance of accounts payable teams. Such analytics are crucial for businesses that rely on data-driven decision-making, allowing them to optimize financial operations and improve overall efficiency.
