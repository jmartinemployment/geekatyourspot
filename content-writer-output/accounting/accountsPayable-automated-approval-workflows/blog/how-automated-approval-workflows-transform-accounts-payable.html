Recognizing the Inefficiencies of Manual Approval Workflows

Many small and medium-sized businesses find themselves caught in a cycle of inefficiencies due to manual approval workflows. The process often involves multiple layers of approval, with invoices moving from desk to desk, waiting for signatures, and possibly getting lost in the shuffle. This approach is not only time-consuming but prone to human error. It's a system that struggles under the weight of growing business demands, leading to delays and frustrations.

Imagine a typical scenario: an invoice arrives and is printed out, then placed into a physical folder. It sits on a manager’s desk, waiting for their review and approval. If the manager is tied up in meetings or out of the office, the invoice just sits there, day after day. When the manager finally gets to it, they might spot an error or have a question, prompting a back-and-forth with the vendor or other departments. This adds further delay.

Moreover, manual processes lack real-time tracking. If a client calls to ask about the status of their invoice, it takes time for someone to physically locate the document and determine its status. This lack of visibility into the process can frustrate both employees and customers alike, damaging relationships and reputation.

In a competitive business environment, time is money. Manual workflows eat into time that could be better spent on strategic tasks. They also tie up resources, as employees spend countless hours on paperwork and data entry. The inefficiency of these processes can be a significant drain on productivity, particularly for businesses trying to scale.

The real cost, however, comes in the form of missed opportunities. As businesses grow, the volume of invoices increases, and manual workflows simply cannot keep up. This results in delayed payments, which can sour vendor relationships and even result in late fees. The longer it takes to process an approval, the longer it takes to make a payment, and the more damage it can do to the bottom line.

The Hidden Costs of Manual Approval Workflows

While the inefficiencies of manual approval workflows are clear, the hidden costs are often overlooked until they become a critical issue. One of the most significant costs is the time lost to manual processes. Each invoice that requires manual handling can take up to 20 minutes to process, from data entry to final approval. Multiply this by hundreds of invoices per month, and the hours quickly add up.

Human error is another costly factor. Errors in data entry are common, leading to incorrect payments or missing information. These mistakes often require additional time to rectify, creating a ripple effect of delays that can affect cash flow and financial reporting. For businesses operating with a slim margin, these errors can have a significant impact on profitability.

Payment delays are perhaps the most tangible cost of manual workflows. Late payments can incur fees, strain vendor relationships, and even lead to a loss of supplier contracts. For small businesses, maintaining good relationships with suppliers is crucial, and any disruption can have long-term consequences.

The administrative burden also falls on your team. Employees tasked with handling approvals are often overworked, leading to burnout and decreased job satisfaction. High turnover rates can result, increasing recruitment and training costs. Furthermore, the morale of a team bogged down by inefficient processes can decline, affecting overall productivity.

Finally, there's the opportunity cost. Manual processes prevent your business from responding quickly to market changes and new opportunities. With resources tied up in time-consuming tasks, there's less capacity for innovation and growth. In today's fast-paced business world, this can put your company at a competitive disadvantage.

How Automated Approval Workflows Change the Accounts Payable Process

Automating the approval workflow for accounts payable transforms the way businesses handle invoices and payments. The transition from manual to automated processes is not just about replacing paper with digital versions; it fundamentally changes the workflow, making it more efficient and less prone to error. Here's how the process unfolds once automation takes over.

First, invoices are captured digitally. Tools like Bill use optical character recognition (OCR) to extract invoice data and enter it directly into the system. This means no more manual data entry, which reduces the risk of human error and speeds up the initial processing phase. With the data captured accurately, invoices are ready for the next step: validation.

Validation ensures that each invoice matches purchase orders and receipts, checking for discrepancies before they cause payment delays. Once validated, the invoice moves automatically into the approval stage. Here, tools like ApprovalMax come into play, setting up predefined rules that route invoices to the right people based on criteria like amount, vendor, or department. This smart routing eliminates the need for manual follow-ups and approvals, making the process faster and more reliable.

Once the invoice is approved, the system moves seamlessly into payment execution. Platforms such as Ramp automate this step by scheduling payments according to predefined schedules, ensuring that vendors are paid on time without the need for manual intervention. This reduces the risk of late payments and improves vendor relationships.

Throughout this entire automated process, tools maintain a detailed audit trail. This trail logs every action taken, providing transparency and accountability. If discrepancies or questions arise, businesses can quickly trace back through the workflow to identify and resolve issues.

In summary, automated approval workflows streamline the accounts payable process by reducing manual errors, speeding up invoice processing, and ensuring timely payments. By leveraging tools like Bill, ApprovalMax, and Ramp, businesses can transform their AP operations from a manual, error-prone task into an efficient, automated workflow.

Deciding When to Automate Approval Workflows

Automating approval workflows isn't a one-size-fits-all solution. The decision depends on the specific needs and circumstances of your business. Here’s how to decide when automation is the right move and when it might not be necessary.

Automation is most beneficial for businesses dealing with high volumes of invoices. If your team is overwhelmed by manual processing, automation can alleviate this burden by routing invoices, reducing processing times, and ensuring compliance. For example, Tipalti offers automated routing and approval workflows that significantly reduce the manual workload and improve efficiency.

However, automation isn't always the best choice for every business. For smaller companies with minimal invoice traffic, the cost and effort of implementing an automated system may outweigh the benefits. Manual processes might suffice when the volume is low and the current system isn't causing significant delays or errors.

Before deciding, consider these questions: How many invoices per month require someone’s approval? Where do invoices currently enter the business? Who approves spending, and what happens if they are unavailable? How often are invoices paid late, paid twice, disputed, or lost? How much time does the bookkeeper spend chasing approvals each week? Can the owner see all bills awaiting approval and all approved bills due in the next 7, 14, and 30 days? Are approval requirements based on amount, department, job/project, entity, vendor, or expense type? Is there an audit trail sufficient to answer “who approved this payment and why?”
