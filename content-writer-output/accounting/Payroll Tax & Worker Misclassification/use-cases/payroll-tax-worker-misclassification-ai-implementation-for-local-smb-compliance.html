People Also Ask

If I have a signed contract stating a worker is an independent contractor, am I safe from a misclassification audit?

No, a signed contract alone does not protect you from a misclassification audit. Agencies like the IRS look at the actual work relationship, not just the paperwork. If a worker should be classified as an employee, you could owe back wages, taxes, and face penalties even if you have a contract saying they are a contractor. It’s important to use a clear, defensible process for classification and review your practices regularly to avoid costly mistakes.

How do the 2026 tax reporting changes affect my 1099-NEC obligations?

Starting in 2026, the federal filing threshold for Form 1099-NEC will rise from $600 to $2,000. This means you only need to send a 1099-NEC to contractors you’ve paid $2,000 or more in a calendar year. For those paid between $600 and $1,999, you will not issue a 1099-NEC, but you should communicate this change clearly to avoid confusion. Also, some states may still require reporting at lower thresholds, so check local rules to stay fully compliant.

Can an automated tool handle the localized Miami-Dade discretionary surtaxes for my remote or traveling employees?

Automated payroll and compliance solutions can help manage local tax rules, including county-level requirements like those in Miami-Dade. These systems are designed to flag compliance issues and recommend actions, making it easier to keep up with federal, state, and local payroll tax obligations—even when your team is remote or on the move. For highly localized surtaxes, it’s important to choose a tool that tracks and updates local requirements as your business grows.

Will implementing an affiliate and partner compliance tool slow down our ability to pay out local contractors?

Modern affiliate and partner compliance tools are built to speed up, not slow down, contractor payments.
